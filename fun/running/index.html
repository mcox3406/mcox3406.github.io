Calculation method and interpretation

Daniels–Gilbert race equivalence: compute a VDOT performance index from one distance and time, then solve for the same index at each other distance. Reference distances are 42.195 km, 21.0975 km, 10 km, 5 km, and 1609.344 m. Values are rounded to the nearest second; 400 m splits use unrounded values.

With velocity v in m/min and duration t in minutes:

C(v) = −4.6 + 0.182258v + 0.000104v²f(t) = 0.8 + 0.1894393e^(−0.012778t) + 0.2989558e^(−0.1932605t)VDOT = C(v) / f(t)

The equations are reproduced in Smyth et al. (2022), Eqs. 7–9. VDOT is a performance index, not measured VO₂max. HMP is a race equivalent, not a measured lactate-threshold pace. Individual endurance, speed, terrain, and conditions affect transfer across distances; uncertainty is particularly relevant when estimating mile performance from a marathon.

Sample plan assumptions and references

The sample covers September 7–November 8, 2026, ahead of CIM on December 6, 2026. Five weeks of speed work and aerobic volume (90, 94, 98, 100, 92 mi/week) lead into the marathon-specific block on October 12 (96, 104, 110, 88 mi/week). The weeks are Monday–Sunday; September 7 is included so the sample begins on a Monday. Sessions before today are planned examples, not recorded activities. The sample ends November 8; the final preparation and race taper are not yet scheduled.

The preparation phase pairs Tuesday 5K/10K-pace intervals with Friday mile-effort repetitions, full jog recoveries, and easy 22–24 mi long runs. Short repetitions emphasize relaxed speed and economy; recovery permits consistent form, following the V.O2 interval/repetition guidance. Weekly volume follows the requested 90–100 mi range. The final preparation week reduces faster-running volume before the marathon-specific sessions.

Haugen et al. (2022) describe world-class marathon training with frequent doubles and a large majority of volume at low intensity. That descriptive review informs the structure; it does not establish an optimal workload for an individual. The 110 mi peak, routine 22–24 mi long runs for race-day confidence, and 22 mi runs finishing with 10 mi slightly faster than MP are user-specified preferences. The longer Sundays redistribute easy weekday mileage to retain the same weekly totals; the 24 mi runs stay entirely easy.

Sample running durations use assumed easy paces and the prescribed faster segments. Easy minutes include warm-ups, recoveries, cool-downs, and easy second runs. This is a planned duration split, not a measured physiological zone distribution. Plans without segment totals fall back to classification by the supplied daily RPE.

Nutrition ranges follow Burke et al. (2019), Contemporary Nutrition Strategies to Optimize Performance in Distance Runners and Race Walkers: 30–60 g carbohydrate/h for continuous exercise lasting 1–2.5 h and up to 90 g/h beyond 2.5 h. Apply by individual session duration and tolerance. These ranges do not determine total energy intake.
